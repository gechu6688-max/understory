# Understory Round 7 - Existing Site Enhancement & Logo Seed Study

Round 7 corrects the Round 6 risk: Understory does not need a new visual territory. The current website is the foundation. This round tests only small mechanisms that may make the existing site feel more uniquely itself.

No live site files were modified for this round. No logo, monogram, symbol, or favicon candidate was created or installed.

## Existing Site Inspected

- Current header: sticky warm surface, lowercase Georgia `understory` wordmark, small sans navigation, language switch, mobile `Menu` details panel.
- Typography: Georgia / Times-like serif for wordmark and major editorial headlines; Inter/system sans for body, nav, metadata, and structured UI.
- Palette: warm page `#F7F4EE`, surface `#FFFDF8`, text `#191714`, secondary `#55514A`, muted `#7A746B`, border `#DED7CA`, brand green `#174D3C`, dark green `#0F352B`.
- Widths and spacing: 64rem nav width, 5xl main width, `--measure-wide: 58rem`, `--measure-article: 45rem`, restrained border/rule rhythm.
- Pages and flows: homepage, cases library, individual case article, topics, concepts, method, about, method sequence, search/discovery pattern.
- Current case flow: status/type/date, large serif title, summary, Central Question, Quick Thesis, taxonomy, article body, references.
- Prior explorations: Rounds 1-5 logo studies and Round 6 brand territories.

## Existing Foundations To Preserve

- Keep the current typography system.
- Keep the lowercase serif `understory` wordmark treatment.
- Keep the current green palette and warm editorial page color.
- Keep current navigation architecture: Cases, Topics, Concepts, Method, About, language switch, mobile menu.
- Keep current content widths and article measure.
- Keep basic editorial hierarchy: eyebrow, title, summary, structured case modules.
- Keep the existing case-study reading flow: question, evidence, counterargument, trade-off, judgement, one more question.
- Keep current interaction style: restrained hover/focus states, underline motion, details-based mobile menu, no dramatic motion.

## Experiment A - Beneath Reveal

Source: Round 6 / Beneath The Surface.

Mechanism tested: a single exposed lower layer beneath the existing Quick Thesis or a section divider. It shows the structure under a surface claim without changing the layout.

- Strength: Most uniquely Understory. It expresses "there is more underneath" through site behavior rather than illustration.
- Risk: If used too often, it could become stacked-card decoration.
- Evaluation: Preserve site 9/10; native feel 8/10; distinctiveness 9/10; analytical clarity 8/10; future symbol potential 9/10.
- Recommendation: Worth further testing in a real case page.

## Experiment B - Evidence Annotation

Source: Round 6 / Field Notes.

Mechanism tested: restrained method markers for Evidence, Counterargument, Judgement, and One More Question. The markers appear only at structural moments, not every paragraph.

- Strength: Improves analytical clarity and supports the site's method.
- Risk: Over-labeling could make Understory feel academic or procedural.
- Evaluation: Preserve site 8/10; native feel 8/10; distinctiveness 8/10; analytical clarity 9/10; future symbol potential 8/10.
- Recommendation: Keep as a selective system device, especially for long cases.

## Experiment C - Second Reading

Source: Round 6 / The Second Reading.

Mechanism tested: one paired first/second interpretation beneath a homepage case item or claim.

- Strength: Communicates the brand idea quickly and memorably.
- Risk: Repeated globally, it becomes a formula and may slow reading.
- Evaluation: Preserve site 8/10; native feel 7/10; distinctiveness 8/10; analytical clarity 8/10; future symbol potential 6/10.
- Recommendation: Reject as a repeated system. Use only as occasional editorial emphasis.

## Experiment D - Editorial Refinement

Source: Round 6 / Editorial Understory.

Mechanism tested: source strip, tighter metadata hierarchy, and slightly more disciplined rule usage.

- Strength: Safest polish; improves trust and scanning while preserving the existing site.
- Risk: Least distinctive. It may make the site better but not more ownable.
- Evaluation: Preserve site 10/10; native feel 9/10; distinctiveness 6/10; reading support 9/10; future symbol potential 7/10.
- Recommendation: Keep as baseline polish, not as the main brand mechanism.

## Potential Logo Seeds Emerging From Existing Site

These are primitive fragments only. They are not finished logos.

### Seed 01 - Exposed Lower Plane

- Source mechanism: Experiment A / Beneath Reveal.
- Site location: Quick Thesis lower layer and case section divider.
- Why native: It directly turns Understory's premise into page behavior: visible surface first, underlying structure second.
- Future symbol potential: Highest. Develop only after the reveal mechanism works in a live case context.

### Seed 02 - Interrupted Editorial Rule

- Source mechanism: Experiment D / Editorial Refinement.
- Site location: source strip, case opener, section rules.
- Why native: The current site already uses rules. A proprietary interruption could be introduced without visual disruption.
- Future symbol potential: Medium. Elegant but possibly too generic alone.

### Seed 03 - Evidence Notch

- Source mechanism: Experiment B / Evidence Annotation.
- Site location: evidence, source, counterargument, judgement markers.
- Why native: Understory's method depends on showing what kind of claim is being made.
- Future symbol potential: Medium-high as a system mark; lower as the primary brand symbol.

### Seed 04 - Offset Second Layer

- Source mechanism: Experiment A plus Experiment C.
- Site location: first claim / underlying structure, first reading / second complication.
- Why native: It suggests asking again through alignment behavior rather than punctuation.
- Future symbol potential: High, but it must avoid becoming a diagram.

### Seed 05 - Margin Continuation

- Source mechanism: Experiment B / Evidence Annotation.
- Site location: selective side markers and future contribution layer.
- Why native: It suggests analysis continuing beyond one answer.
- Future symbol potential: Medium. Better as contribution-layer language for now.

### Seed 06 - Claim / Counterclaim Crossing

- Source mechanism: Experiment C / Second Reading.
- Site location: paired interpretation blocks.
- Why native: It shows an alternative explanation crossing the initial claim.
- Future symbol potential: Low-medium. Conceptually useful, but visually more invented than site-born.

## Final Recommendation

### A. Which enhancement should definitely be rejected?

Reject Experiment C as a repeated global pattern. It can be used occasionally, but it should not become the main site mechanism.

### B. Which one or two enhancements are genuinely worth keeping?

Keep Experiment A / Beneath Reveal and Experiment B / Evidence Annotation for further testing. Use Experiment D as quiet baseline polish.

### C. Which mechanism feels most uniquely Understory?

Experiment A / Beneath Reveal. It expresses "go beneath the obvious" without metaphor, icons, or new visual territory.

### D. Which logo seed feels most naturally born from the existing site?

Seed 01 / Exposed Lower Plane. It emerges from the strongest site behavior rather than from a separate mark-making exercise.

### E. What should happen next?

Test Experiment A and B inside one real case page mockup or branch before starting logo development. Do not proceed directly to a finished symbol yet. If the reveal mechanism feels native in a real article, then develop Seed 01 into a symbol exploration.
