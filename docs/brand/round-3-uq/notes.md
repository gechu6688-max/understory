# Understory Round 3 — U / Q Integration Study

Round 3 intentionally breaks from the Round 2 `U + subtle question gesture + dot` grammar. The goal is not to draw `UQ`; it is to find one visual structure where Understory and Question can coexist. These marks prioritize silhouette first, concept second.

Existing system references used before designing:

- Current wordmark: lowercase `understory`, Georgia / Times-like serif, semibold
- Brand green: `#174D3C`
- Deep green: `#0F352B`
- Page: `#F7F4EE`
- Surface: `#FFFDF8`
- Text: `#191714`
- Border: `#DED7CA`
- Header: sticky, warm surface, restrained nav, animated underline, `max-width: 64rem`

Round 1 and Round 2 files were inspected and left untouched.

## R3-A — U Becomes Q

Concept: A compact U form becomes Q through the right side’s necessary exit stroke. The tail is not attached as decoration; it reads as the continuation of analysis into the next question.

- Strength: Strong silhouette, easiest to read as a mature standalone mark.
- Risk: The U and Q readings are relatively direct, so it needs careful wordmark spacing to avoid feeling like a literal lettermark.
- Brand distinctiveness: Medium-high; strong enough for header and social use, but the U-to-Q idea is familiar territory.
- Favicon performance: Strong at 32px and 24px; acceptable at 16px because it is mostly one filled silhouette.
- U readable: Yes, immediately.
- Q readable: Yes, after noticing the exit tail.
- Explicit letter readability necessary: No. The filled form still works as a compact analytical symbol.
- Blur test: Passes; the broad U bowl and diagonal tail remain visible.
- Memory test: Passes; most viewers could redraw “U with one tail.”

## R3-B — Hidden U Inside Q

Concept: A complete Q-like outer silhouette contains a U in negative space. This is the most “beneath the obvious” direction: the first read is a self-contained mark, while the U is discovered only on closer inspection.

- Strength: Strongest conceptual fit for hidden structure and underlying systems.
- Risk: At very small sizes the negative-space U can close up, making the mark read as a dense Q.
- Brand distinctiveness: High; less like a simple monogram because the U is not drawn as a separate letter.
- Favicon performance: Good at 32px, acceptable at 24px, weaker at 16px unless the negative-space U is simplified.
- U readable: Subtle, discovered.
- Q readable: Yes, from the outer silhouette and tail.
- Explicit letter readability necessary: No. This direction benefits if the U is sensed rather than spelled out.
- Blur test: Passes as a Q-like mass, with the U becoming secondary.
- Memory test: Passes moderately; viewers remember “solid Q with a cutout.”

## R3-C — Shared Stroke U/Q

Concept: One continuous geometric path creates both readings. It starts as a U, folds into an internal Q logic, and exits through a controlled tail. This is the closest to “one structure, two readings.”

- Strength: Most elegant integration of U and Q as one uninterrupted thought.
- Risk: Because it is stroke-based, it can still feel glyph-like if pushed too thin or too calligraphic.
- Brand distinctiveness: Medium; the path logic is thoughtful, but the mark needs optical tuning to avoid looking like a drawn character.
- Favicon performance: Good at 32px, acceptable at 24px, borderline at 16px.
- U readable: Yes.
- Q readable: Partial; it is more implied than explicit.
- Explicit letter readability necessary: No. The continuity matters more than letter clarity.
- Blur test: Mixed; the overall path survives, but internal logic softens.
- Memory test: Moderate; easier to remember as “single stroke U that exits.”

## R3-D — Interlocking U/Q

Concept: U and Q share one structural body. A vertical channel, curve, and negative-space cut produce an interlocked reading without placing two initials side by side. The diagonal cut prevents the mark from becoming a static institutional monogram.

- Strength: Most compact and favicon-oriented of the interlocking directions.
- Risk: The solid geometry may edge toward an institutional abbreviation if the countershape is not tuned carefully.
- Brand distinctiveness: Medium-high; memorable block silhouette with a specific internal cut.
- Favicon performance: Strong at 32px and 24px; acceptable at 16px.
- U readable: Yes, through the inner channel and bottom return.
- Q readable: Yes, through the outer body and diagonal cut/tail.
- Explicit letter readability necessary: Not fully. It works best if the shared structure is recognized before the initials.
- Blur test: Passes; the block mass and diagonal exit remain.
- Memory test: Passes; simple enough to redraw as “block U/Q with cut.”
