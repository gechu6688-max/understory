Understory / Round 8

Exposed lower plane mechanism study.

Boundary

This is not logo design. The study isolates a recurring visual behavior: a surface remains primary while a lower analytical layer becomes visible through edge, slit, cut, crossing, interruption, asymmetry, or baseline shift.

01 / Diagnostic

Keep the mechanism. Remove the callout-box feeling.

Round 7 proved that a lower layer feels native to Understory when it reveals structure beneath a claim. The weak point was the boxiness: the exposed layer could read like a note card or UI callout. Round 8 reduces the idea into edges, cuts, rules, and baselines.

Natural

Surface claim plus underlying condition.

It directly maps to the existing case flow: central question, thesis, evidence, counterargument, judgement.

Too box-like

Large tinted rectangles become callouts.

The lower layer should be visible without becoming another card or dashboard module.

Preserve

Current rules, measure, green, serif voice.

The site already has enough structure. The mechanism should use existing grammar.

Avoid

Badges, app icons, perfect symmetry.

The behavior must first work as editorial layout, not compact symbol geometry.

02 / M1-M8 Overview

Eight ways for the lower plane to become visible.

Each mechanism is shown as a large, medium, and header-scale primitive fragment. These are fragments, not logo candidates.

03 / Case Header

The mechanism around claim and thesis.

Use real Understory case logic: the headline stays primary; the lower plane only exposes the condition beneath the thesis.

04 / Divider

Section transitions without new page architecture.

The current site already uses editorial rules. The question is whether a lower plane can make those rules feel more proprietary.

05 / Evidence & Source

Evidence beneath the visible claim.

These applications test source notes, hidden assumptions, and counterarguments without turning the article into annotated clutter.

06 / Caption & Metadata

Lower planes inside editorial metadata.

Caption, date, category, source label, and verification status are ideal places to test subtle exposure without interrupting reading.

07 / Repeatability & Ownership

Can this become a system, not a one-off trick?

Each mechanism is judged on repeatability, risk of annoyance, fit across content types, and whether it could belong too easily to another category.

08 / Final Ranking

Website behavior first. Symbol potential second.

Scores are directional. A high symbol score does not mean the fragment is ready to become a logo; it means the behavior may later support one.

RejectM2 Offset Reveal as a primary system. It drifts fastest toward stacked-card UI and dashboard logic.
Strongest behaviorsM1 Edge Reveal and M8 Baseline Shift. They are subtle, repeatable, and native to editorial layouts.
Most uniquely UnderstoryM1 Edge Reveal: a tiny lower edge can make the visible claim feel incomplete in the right way.
Best future fragmentM3 Slit Reveal has the most memorable primitive mark, but should remain a mechanism for now.
Ready for logo?None are fully ready. M1 and M3 deserve another applied round before symbol development resumes.
