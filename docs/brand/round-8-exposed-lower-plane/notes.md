# Understory Round 8 - Exposed Lower Plane Mechanism Study

Round 8 studies one narrow behavior before any logo work resumes: a visible surface with a lower analytical plane exposed beneath it.

No live site files were modified. No U/Q marks, monograms, logo candidates, or favicon candidates were created.

## Current Site And Round 7 Inspected

- Current header: sticky, warm surface, lowercase Georgia `understory` wordmark, small sans navigation, language switch, and mobile details menu.
- Current typography: Georgia / Times-like serif for the wordmark and major headlines; Inter/system sans for body, metadata, navigation, and structured UI.
- Current palette: warm paper `#F7F4EE`, off-white surface `#FFFDF8`, text `#191714`, border `#DED7CA`, brand green `#174D3C`.
- Current structure: 64rem header, 5xl main width, 58rem case header, 45rem article measure, restrained editorial rules.
- Current case pattern: status/date/type, large title, summary, Central Question, Quick Thesis, taxonomy, article body, references.
- Round 7 reviewed: Beneath Reveal experiment and logo seed study.

## Diagnostic: Keep The Mechanism. Remove The Callout-Box Feeling.

What felt natural in Round 7:

- A surface claim with an exposed underlying condition maps directly to Understory's premise.
- The mechanism fits the current case flow: claim, evidence, counterargument, judgement, one more question.
- It can appear in the site without a new visual territory.

What felt too much like an info box:

- Large tinted lower rectangles started to read as callout cards.
- The lower layer risked becoming a dashboard module rather than editorial behavior.
- If the exposed layer is too complete, it stops feeling hidden or discovered.

What should be preserved:

- Existing typography, wordmark, palette, widths, and rule system.
- The sense that the lower layer is secondary but structurally important.
- The idea that evidence or condition sits beneath the visible claim.

What should be avoided:

- Stacked-card UI.
- Generic drop-shadow logic.
- Rounded-square icon thinking.
- Thick enclosed silhouettes.
- Q-like tails or monogram implications.
- Reveals that require explanation to look intentional.

## M1 - Edge Reveal

A lower layer becomes visible only along one edge.

- Best uses: case thesis edge, divider underside, source metadata edge.
- Repeatability: high.
- Brand ownership: strong when tied to claim/evidence structure.
- Risk: too subtle if the exposed edge is not used consistently.
- Symbol potential: high, but not ready for logo development yet.

## M2 - Offset Reveal

The lower plane is shifted slightly from the upper surface.

- Best uses: one-off structural diagrams or special case openers.
- Repeatability: low-medium.
- Brand ownership: weak; it can easily look like SaaS/dashboard card stacking.
- Risk: strongest app-icon and UI-card bias.
- Recommendation: reject as a primary system.

## M3 - Slit Reveal

A narrow opening exposes something beneath.

- Best uses: key divider, source opening, analytical turn.
- Repeatability: medium-high if used sparingly.
- Brand ownership: strong and memorable.
- Risk: can look like a loading bar or menu mark if too linear.
- Symbol potential: strongest primitive fragment, but needs more applied testing.

## M4 - Undercut

Part of the upper surface is removed, letting the lower layer show.

- Best uses: evidence entry point, source note, uncertainty notch.
- Repeatability: medium.
- Brand ownership: moderate.
- Risk: can become a generic ticket notch or speech-bubble cue.
- Symbol potential: medium.

## M5 - Lower Plane Crossing

The underlying layer crosses or escapes beyond the primary surface.

- Best uses: counterargument crossing a claim, lower source line extending past a caption.
- Repeatability: medium.
- Brand ownership: conceptually strong.
- Risk: can drift toward arrows, tails, or earlier Q-like logic.
- Symbol potential: medium-high but risky.

## M6 - Interrupted Surface

The top layer appears continuous, but one section is displaced or missing.

- Best uses: rule grammar, section transitions, uncertainty marks.
- Repeatability: high.
- Brand ownership: good but quiet.
- Risk: may be too understated to become memorable alone.
- Symbol potential: medium.

## M7 - Asymmetric Exposure

The lower plane is revealed unevenly.

- Best uses: non-centered evidence traces and one-sided metadata exposure.
- Repeatability: medium.
- Brand ownership: good if asymmetry follows content logic.
- Risk: can become decorative if not systematized.
- Symbol potential: medium-high.

## M8 - Baseline Shift

The second layer appears through typographic or structural baseline shift.

- Best uses: captions, metadata, source status, small analytical phrases.
- Repeatability: very high.
- Brand ownership: strong as editorial behavior.
- Risk: less visually iconic and less symbol-ready.
- Symbol potential: low-medium, but excellent website behavior.

## Final Ranking

1. M1 - Edge Reveal
2. M8 - Baseline Shift
3. M3 - Slit Reveal
4. M6 - Interrupted Surface
5. M7 - Asymmetric Exposure
6. M5 - Lower Plane Crossing
7. M4 - Undercut
8. M2 - Offset Reveal

## Answers

### A. Which mechanism should definitely be rejected?

Reject M2 Offset Reveal as a primary system. It is too close to stacked-card UI and dashboard logic.

### B. Which two mechanisms are strongest as website behaviors?

M1 Edge Reveal and M8 Baseline Shift. M1 is visually distinctive while still subtle; M8 is the most naturally editorial and repeatable.

### C. Which mechanism is most uniquely Understory?

M1 Edge Reveal. It makes a visible claim feel incomplete in a way that directly supports "go beneath the obvious."

### D. Which primitive fragment is most promising for future symbol development?

M3 Slit Reveal has the strongest isolated fragment, while M1 has the strongest site-born behavior. The best future direction may combine M1's site behavior with M3's controlled opening.

### E. Is any fragment truly ready to become a logo seed?

Not yet. M1 and M3 are promising, but they need one more applied round inside real article components before symbol development resumes.
