Territory C / The Second Reading

Look once. Then again.

A system of paired readings: claim and counterclaim, first impression and underlying interpretation, visible benefit and hidden trade-off.

First reading

Low prices benefit consumers.

Fast delivery, wide selection, and easy comparison reduce friction.

Second reading

Low prices are not the whole answer.

Market power can form inside convenience when rivals weaken and sellers become dependent.

Evidence: legal complaint is not a finding.
Counterargument: efficiency may explain benefits.
Question: what evidence changes the conclusion?

Argument: convenience can lower friction while increasing dependence.

Evidence: Amazon’s public filings and regulatory allegations point to a complex platform system.

Judgement: short-term benefit should not end the analysis.

Counter-reading: effectiveness is not automatically exclusion.

Scale, logistics, seller competition, and better technology may explain many consumer benefits.
