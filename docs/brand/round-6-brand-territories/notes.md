# Understory Round 6 — Brand Territory Before Symbol

Round 6 is a deliberate pause from logo design. Phase one contains no logo symbols, U/Q marks, monograms, favicon candidates, or lettermarks. The goal is to define the visual world from which a future symbol could naturally emerge.

## Current Site Context Inspected

- Homepage: restrained editorial homepage with warm paper background, Georgia serif headlines, structured case discovery, method sequence, and green rules.
- Header: sticky warm surface, lowercase Georgia `understory` wordmark, small sans nav, restrained hover underline.
- Typography: Georgia / Times-like serif for identity and major headlines; Inter/system sans for body, nav, metadata, and structured UI text.
- Color: `#F7F4EE` page, `#FFFDF8` surface, `#191714` text, `#55514A` secondary text, `#DED7CA` border, `#174D3C` green.
- Pages inspected: Home, Cases, Topics, Concepts, Method, About, case article template, and Amazon draft case.
- Rounds 1-5 inspected: symbol-first exploration, U/Q integration, B/D refinement, and anti-monogram reset.

## Why Rounds 1-5 Did Not Fully Land

- Too much symbol-first thinking before a broader editorial system existed.
- Over-explaining the concept through geometry: U, Q, tails, cutouts, layers, descent.
- U/Q construction began dominating the identity instead of supporting it.
- Favicon optimization shaped the logo too early, creating compact app-icon pressure.
- Thick closed silhouettes pushed some marks toward SaaS, fintech, or app icon territory.
- The mark was insufficiently connected to the actual reading system: cases, evidence, counterargument, judgement, references.
- Many variations were construction variants rather than true brand worlds.

## Territory A — Field Notes

Understory as a living analytical notebook: margin rails, evidence pins, source notes, counterargument marks, and one-more-question prompts. It feels human and iterative without becoming messy or student-like.

- Strongest for contribution-layer growth.
- Best recurring devices: evidence pin, citation rail, margin prompt.
- Future symbol seed: margin interruption or proprietary annotation mark.
- Risk: can become too procedural if the margin system overpowers the prose.

## Territory B — Beneath The Surface

The visual system reveals what sits below the primary claim: hidden metadata, lower planes, exposed captions, and shifted surfaces. This territory most directly owns the Understory premise without using literal underground imagery.

- Hardest to rename as another brand.
- Best expression of “Go beneath. Ask again.” without illustrating it literally.
- Future symbol seed: exposed lower plane, reveal edge, structural cut.
- Risk: more spatial complexity may reduce long-form reading comfort if overused.

## Territory C — The Second Reading

The first read is never the final read. Claim and counterclaim, first impression and second interpretation, visible benefit and hidden trade-off are paired in the structure.

- Strongest 5-second visual memory.
- Best recurring devices: first/second split, reversal divider, embedded counterclaim.
- Future symbol seed: reversible divider or figure-ground mechanism.
- Risk: conceptual tension must not become gimmicky or slow down reading.

## Territory D — Editorial Understory

Understory as a contemporary analytical publication: case indices, source plates, strong openers, refined rules, and controlled green. This is the safest long-term reading system and closest evolution of the current site.

- Best long-form reading support.
- Best fit with current site.
- Best editorial intelligence score.
- Future symbol seed: index marker, section sign, publication imprint element.
- Risk: must avoid becoming a generic magazine template.

## Initial Strategic Read

- Best overall future platform world: **Territory B — Beneath The Surface**.
- Best immediate site evolution: **Territory D — Editorial Understory**.
- Best contribution-layer foundation: **Territory A — Field Notes**.
- Most memorable conceptual system: **Territory C — The Second Reading**.

The strongest future direction may combine B’s reveal logic, D’s publication discipline, and A’s contribution-ready annotation system.
