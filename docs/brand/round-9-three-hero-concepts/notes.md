# Understory Round 9 - Three Hero Logo Concepts

Round 9 returns to actual logo design after Rounds 1-8 clarified what should be avoided. The priority changed deliberately:

1. Strong form first.
2. Hidden story second.
3. Brand explanation only deepens an already attractive mark.

No live site files were modified. No logo was installed. The current wordmark, typography, grid, navigation, and palette remain unchanged.

## Context Inspected

- Current website foundation: warm paper page, off-white surface, Georgia serif wordmark, Inter/system body, restrained green, 64rem header, 58rem case header, 45rem article measure.
- Round 7 conclusion: Beneath Reveal and Evidence Annotation were the strongest site mechanisms; Exposed Lower Plane was the most natural future seed.
- Round 8 conclusion: M1 Edge Reveal and M8 Baseline Shift are strongest website behaviors; M3 Slit Reveal has the strongest primitive fragment. None were ready to become a logo directly.
- Rounds 1-6 lesson: avoid obvious U/Q marks, question marks, monogram logic, app-icon containers, ancient-glyph feeling, and concept-first geometry that lacks emotional pull.

## Phase 1 - Black And White Only

Nine candidates were created:

### A - The Folded Question

Core idea: curiosity opens the surface and reveals a lower structure.

- A1 Lifted Surface: sculptural surface with a lifted diagonal facet.
- A2 Internal Turn: stable outer form with an internal plane turning inward.
- A3 Revealed Counterform: strong emblem where the inner counterform changes the reading.

### B - The Hidden Thread

Core idea: the thread does not end where it disappears.

- B1 Disappearing Thread: compact structure with trace segments that disappear and reappear.
- B2 Embedded Trace: thread created mainly through negative space inside the main form.
- B3 Trace Through Depth: path passes behind the form, leaving selected visible segments.

### C - The Unfinished Emblem

Core idea: not unfinished because it lacks an answer, but because one answer is not the whole story.

- C1 Intentional Gap: controlled unresolved gap.
- C2 Displaced Completion: completion exists offset on another level.
- C3 Unresolved Join: two parts nearly connect, creating tension.

## Top 3 Selected

### Top 1 - A3 Revealed Counterform

- First glance: faceted emblem with a strong inner event.
- Second look: the inner chamber changes the whole outer form.
- Understory: visible shell is not the full story.
- 问溯: the opened counterform invites tracing inward.
- About: curiosity creates a chamber for thinking.
- Strength: strongest standalone brand presence.
- Risk: may need refinement to reduce architectural/object association.

### Top 2 - B2 Embedded Trace

- First glance: compact editorial slab with a confident cut structure.
- Second look: a trace appears as negative space, not a drawn line.
- Understory: evidence below the claim.
- 问溯: a thread continues where it disappears.
- About: one question creates the next step.
- Strength: strongest hidden story and most personal fit.
- Risk: could become too rational if the outer silhouette is not warmed up.

### Top 3 - C3 Unresolved Join

- First glance: angular emblem with a tense near-connection.
- Second look: the detached lower piece is not missing; it is almost joining.
- Understory: one answer is not the whole structure.
- 问: openness and refusal of premature closure.
- About: thinking remains alive and unfinished.
- Strength: strongest unresolved tension.
- Risk: highest chance of looking constructed or fragmented rather than inevitable.

## Answers

### Which one feels most like a real brand?

A3 Revealed Counterform.

### Which one has the strongest hidden story?

B2 Embedded Trace.

### Which one feels most personal to Understory?

B2 Embedded Trace, because it best connects to one question leading to another and tracing back.

### Which one would be hardest for another brand to use?

A3 Revealed Counterform, because its inner chamber and faceted outer shell feel less category-generic than the others.

### Which one has the best chance of becoming iconic with repetition?

A3 first, B2 second. C3 has energy, but needs more refinement before it can become confidently memorable.

## Recommendation

Do not install any mark yet.

Advance A3 and B2 into a refinement round. Keep C3 as a useful tension study, but do not let it lead unless it can be made less fragmented. The next round should not create more unrelated concepts; it should refine A3 and B2 with small-size performance, optical correction, and a more mature wordmark relationship.
